Also de-emphasise generic "full-stack developer" framing, pure frontend work, and the academic ML/data-science research as a headline — keep the research as supporting evidence for AI-adjacent roles (#17, #21, #22) rather than as the identity.

* **Dice** — one query run (`backend engineer Go`, Tel Aviv). Returned 16 results, **all US-based or US-remote** (Kforce, Bayside, Atlassian SF, Fieldwire, Vercel, dv01). Dice's `searchedLocation` acknowledged `IL` but returned no Israeli inventory. Confirmed unusable for this search; not queried again.
* **ZipRecruiter** — one query run. Returned a hard error: `Country code "IL" is not supported. Only "CA" and "US" are allowed.` Structurally incapable of covering this market. Not queried again.
* **Indeed** — used heavily and productively (10 searches, 9 detail fetches). Note that Indeed's Israeli inventory carries a long tail of stale listings: a large share of returned postings dated to February–May 2026 and were excluded on freshness grounds.

**Sources that blocked or failed:**
* **jobs.lever.co** — returned **HTTP 403** on a direct job fetch (Lendbuzz Backend Engineer, Tel Aviv). A promising Lever-hosted Tel Aviv backend role surfaced in search results but **could not be verified**, so it is not in the table. Lever job pages appear to be blocking automated fetch this run; only their search-engine snippets were reachable, and a snippet is not verification.
* **Figma's Greenhouse board** — the direct Greenhouse job URL 302-redirected to `figma.com/careers` with an `?error=true` parameter, i.e. the specific req was not retrievable there. The Tel Aviv role was ultimately verified via Built In instead (5+ years, rejected on experience bar).
* **Riskified's Greenhouse board** — 302-redirected to their own careers site; not followed through this run, so Riskified's Israeli openings are unverified.
* **LinkedIn Jobs, Drushim, AllJobs, GotFriends, Nisha, Ethosia, SQLINK, JobMaster** — not directly fetchable, as in every prior run. Attempts to reach them via search-engine indexing returned only aggregator pages (Glassdoor, TheMuse, web3.career) rather than pivotable company postings. **This remains the largest single blind spot in the report**, and it disproportionately hides Israeli agency-represented and Hebrew-language roles.
* **Y Combinator's Israel jobs page** — fetched successfully but returned effectively nothing for Israel: one Tel Aviv/New York hybrid listing at Varos and two roles actually located in Jordan. Not a useful channel this run.
* **Glassdoor** — surfaced repeatedly in search results with large Israeli job counts (1,720 software engineer jobs in Israel), but individual postings were not retrievable and Glassdoor aggregates rather than originates. No role was taken from it.

**Structural gaps in the tooling:**
* **Built In's Israel filter is partial.** Only certain category slugs are actually Israel-scoped (`/search/software-engineer`, `/search/artificial-intelligence`). The slugs `/search/backend`, `/search/full-stack`, `/search/python`, `/search/cloud`, `/search/golang` and the bare `/jobs/mena/israel/dev-engineering` all **silently fall back to US inventory** while keeping `mena/israel` in the URL. Four of those were fetched before the pattern was identified and returned no Israeli jobs at all. Israel coverage was therefore obtained by paging `/search/software-engineer` (pages 1–4) rather than by keyword — meaning **backend and full-stack roles in Israel that are not titled "Software Engineer" are systematically under-sampled by this run.**

**Segments not covered:**
* **Israeli VC portfolio boards** (Aleph, TLV Partners, Vintage, Viola) were not reached this run — search budget went to verifying the carryover set and paging Built In. Team8's portfolio was partially covered incidentally (two Reindeer roles, both 5+ years).
* **Startup Nation Central and Wellfound** were not queried this run.
* **Israeli-language postings** (Hebrew-only listings on local boards) are effectively invisible to this pipeline, which biases the report toward multinationals and English-first startups. This is a persistent, structural gap, not a one-run miss.
