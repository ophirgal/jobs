## 🔹 Positioning recommendation

Lead as a **Backend Engineer specializing in Go, Python and Kubernetes for scalable cloud services, with AI-product engineering experience.** The three assets that carry the most weight, in order:

1. **Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway at 100k+ requests/day.** This is the differentiator. Most engineers with 2–3 years have deployed *to* Kubernetes; building controllers *on* the Kubernetes API is materially rarer, and it satisfies the hard Kubernetes requirements at Stream.Security, Zero Networks, Upwind, AppDome and Google Platform Engineering outright.
2. **Production systems processing 100k+ documents/day at Qualtrics.** Concrete throughput evidence for every "high-scale", "high-throughput" and "distributed systems" requirement in the table — CommIT, DoiT and Bringg all ask for exactly this and phrase it differently.
3. **Generative-AI system evaluation plus text analytics, classification and summarization.** This is what turns the candidate from qualified into differentiated at NYT, Jeen.ai, BrainPOP and the Google SOAR/SecOps AI workstreams. BrainPOP names "AI evaluation frameworks" in the job description itself — quote it back to them.

Add **MSc in Computer Science, University of Maryland (3.9 GPA)** prominently on the three Google applications, where it is a named preferred qualification and, for the SWE III role, is what makes the minimum qualification satisfiable at all.

**De-emphasize:** freelance and vocational teaching — it should appear as evidence of communication and mentoring ability, never positioned as engineering tenure, and never in a way that reads as padding 2–3 years into more. Also de-emphasize pure frontend and CSS/UI work (it invites misfiling into frontend roles), DevOps-flavored framing (the target is software engineering, not operations), and academic ML research framing, which pulls toward the data-science roles that are explicitly out of scope. Do not present as Senior — every High-probability role here is mid-level, and self-titling upward is the fastest way to fail a screen.
