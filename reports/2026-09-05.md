## 🔹 Positioning Recommendation

Lead as a **Backend Engineer specialising in Go, Python and Kubernetes for scalable cloud services, with production generative-AI experience** — not a generic full-stack developer. The single most differentiating line in this profile is *Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway handling 100k+ requests/day*: it is infrastructure-grade backend work, it is quantified, and almost no engineer at the 2–3 year mark can claim it. Pair it with the 100k+ documents/day processing at Qualtrics so the throughput story reads as a pattern, not a single project.

This week's table has an unusually heavy AI-engineering component — Skai, Aikido, Lemonade, Windward and Onshore are five of fifteen — so the second half of the narrative matters more than usual. Frame the generative-AI work as **evaluation and production reliability**, not model building: evals, scorers, tracing, and proving a change improved quality. That framing is what separates the candidate from the much larger pool of engineers who have merely called an LLM API, and it is stated almost verbatim in the Aikido and Onshore reqs.

Sequence as: **(1)** Go/Python distributed backend on Kubernetes and AWS → **(2)** generative-AI evaluation and AI-enabled product work → **(3)** TypeScript/React full-stack ownership → **(4)** MSc in CS at 3.9, with the teaching record as evidence of communication and mentoring.

**De-emphasise:** the freelance teaching years as though they were engineering tenure — cite them as communication and mentoring evidence only, and hold the professional-experience figure at 2–3 years so it is never read as inflated. Also de-emphasise pure ML/data-science research framing (it invites research-scientist screens the candidate does not want), generic "full-stack developer" phrasing, and HTML/CSS-level frontend detail. Where a posting names a language the candidate lacks (C#, Kotlin, Java, Node.js), address it in one line as transferable and return immediately to the Go/Python/Kubernetes evidence.
