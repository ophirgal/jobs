**Lead with:** *Backend / platform software engineer — Go and Python on Kubernetes, building and operating globally distributed, high-throughput cloud services and the internal tooling around them, with hands-on generative-AI evaluation in production.*

Three concrete proof points, in this order, because they are what the fifteen reqs above actually ask for:

1. **Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway, 100k+ requests/day.** This is the single most reusable line in the set — it is the literal scope of Ramp Production Engineering, Temporal Cloud Enablement, Google Chronicle SOAR's provisioning service, and AppsFlyer SaaSLab's API-gateway/auth control plane. Lead with it for every platform and infrastructure-software req.
2. **Production applications processing 100k+ documents/day**, plus root-cause analysis that reduced algorithm errors. This is the "high-scale, correctness, observability, production debugging" evidence that Ramp Credit, Garner III and Stigg are each asking for in different words.
3. **Generative-AI system improvement and evaluation** — framed as *production engineering*, never as research. Palo Alto's "prompting techniques and context management strategies," Appdome's GPT-based test generation, Ramp's agentic workflows and ScyllaDB's AI-assisted development are all engineering asks, and the candidate's experience is engineering experience.

Add **full-stack with backend ownership** as a secondary register — TypeScript, React, HTML/CSS from the Qualtrics work — for Garner, ScyllaDB and Stigg. Keep it secondary; it is a widening of the profile, not its centre.

**De-emphasise:**
- **Freelance / vocational teaching years.** They belong in the résumé as evidence of communication, mentoring and technical explanation — qualities half these reqs name explicitly — but never as years of professional software engineering. Keep the professional count honestly at 2–3 years. Overstating it is the fastest way to lose a 2+ year req to a credibility question.
- **Academic ML and data-science research.** Mention only where it is load-bearing (Google's advanced-degree substitution, text-analytics relevance). Foregrounding it invites misfiling as a data-science or research candidate, which is explicitly not the target.
- **Frontend depth.** CSS, design systems and UI implementation should stay off the front page entirely; the target is backend-weighted, and frontend prominence costs more than it gains.
- **"Generalist full-stack developer" framing.** This profile is sharper than that and competes badly in the generalist pool. Lead with Go/Python/Kubernetes/scale every time.
