## 🔧 Infrastructure fix: the EU Greenhouse blind spot is now partly solved

The last three runs reported that `boards-api.eu.greenhouse.io` is refused by the egress proxy (`connect_rejected`), which blocked per-job reads for EU-hosted Israeli boards. Confirmed again today — the proxy status endpoint logs the denial explicitly.

**Two working routes around it, found this run:**

1. `job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/<slug>` (the *rendering* host, not the API host) returns **HTTP 200** and lists every job ID on an EU board.
2. For ScaleOps specifically, the **non-EU** `boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/scaleops/jobs/<id>` serves the full job content, even though the public link is EU-hosted.

That is how ScaleOps' requirements were read directly this run instead of inferred. Worth trying on any EU-hosted Israeli board next run.

## 🔹 Positioning Recommendation

**Lead with:** *Backend / Full-Stack Engineer — Go and Python, Kubernetes, scalable cloud services, and AI-enabled products.*

Build every application around three concrete, quantified artifacts, in this order:

1. **Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway, 100k+ requests/day.** The strongest single line in the profile and the thing that separates the candidate from the large pool of generic mid-level full-stack developers. It alone qualifies them for platform, infrastructure and control-plane roles (Appdome, Stigg, ScaleOps, MongoDB Atlas, Google Cloud Networking). **New framing worth adopting this run:** for AWS Transform and Flytrex, describe it as *distributed orchestration and reconciliation* rather than as request throughput — both postings are about coordinating many stateful things correctly, not about serving traffic fast.
2. **Production pipelines processing 100k+ documents/day.** The scale credential and the bridge to data-intensive and high-throughput requirements (Robinhood, Stripe, AppsFlyer).
3. **Improving and evaluating generative-AI systems**, with text analytics, classification, sentiment and summarisation. The genuine differentiator in a market where every posting now lists AI tooling. Lead with this for AWS Transform, Comet, Pinecone and ID.me's AI-platform half; mention it everywhere else as evidence of current practice rather than as a specialism.

Add the **MSc in Computer Science (UMD, 3.9)** wherever a posting offers an advanced-degree experience waiver — Google's req converts it directly into a lower bar, and ScaleOps names a top-institution CS degree as a credential.

**One tactical note specific to this run's new finds:** three of the strongest roles (AWS Transform, Flytrex, Stripe Link) publish **stack-agnostic requirements** — no mandatory language. For those, the CV should not foreground the technology list at all; it should foreground **ownership, scale and debugging depth**, because those are the only axes the posting actually screens on. Reserve the Go/Python/Kubernetes keyword density for the reqs that name them (Upwind, Check Point, SailPoint, ScaleOps, MongoDB, ID.me).

**De-emphasise:**

* **Freelance and teaching years as engineering tenure.** Keep teaching visible as evidence of communication, mentoring and technical explanation — it is a genuine strength, and it maps directly onto the "mentor engineers" responsibility in both the Stripe and MongoDB postings — but never let it blur the headline number. The honest claim is **~2–3 years of full-time professional software engineering**, and overstating it is the fastest way to lose credibility in a screen.
* **Frontend-first framing.** React/CSS/UI polish should support a backend-ownership story, not lead it. The exception is Check Point, where a genuine full-stack balance is the ask.
* **Senior/lead vocabulary.** Do not claim architectural or organisational leadership. On the reqs with elevated expectations (MongoDB's "led development work," SailPoint's "millions of identities," ScaleOps' 4+ yrs, AWS Transform's "2+ years of design or architecture"), compete on demonstrated end-to-end ownership of features and on debugging/root-cause depth — both are real and both are checkable.
* **Pure research or data-science positioning.** The ML/data-science research background is an asset *in service of* production AI engineering. Framed as research interest, it pushes the candidate toward the roles this search deliberately excludes — and this run surfaced plenty of them (DoubleVerify, Forter, Fetcherr, Second Nature all had senior data-science reqs open).

* **devjobs.co.il was the most productive source again, and this run I fixed how it is queried.** Prior runs scraped only three grids. Today I found the real filter parameter (`/jobs-grid?developerTypes=<Type>`) and — importantly — **diagnosed a bug in my own prior approach**: the grid URLs were being double-encoded (`AI%2FML` → `AI%252FML`), so the filter was silently ignored and every "filtered" grid was returning the same unfiltered recent-jobs list. With that fixed I scraped the **AI/ML, Cloud/DevOps, Backend, Full Stack and Software** grids properly, filtered on the years-of-experience value printed on each card. That closed the prior run's largest Israel gap and produced the Ecoplant and Yahoo leads.

### Washington, DC — searched actively and seriously; one role included ✅

This is the most thoroughly searched DC has been across runs, and the result is a **structural finding rather than a scarcity excuse**.

* **Dice (DC, 30-mile radius, last 7 days, direct-hire only, sorted by date):** **90 results.** I reviewed the first 25 in detail. **Not one clears the gate**, and they fail in four recognisable clusters:
  1. **Security-clearance-gated** — "TS/SCI with Polygraph" or DoD Secret (Akina SWE 3 Python/Kubernetes/RAG, CACI, General Dynamics, Captivation, BigBear.ai). On paper the Akina "Software Engineer 3 — Python, Kubernetes, RAG" is a striking content match; it requires **TS/SCI with polygraph**, and clearance eligibility is **not established** by the Candidate Reference. Excluded on unresolved eligibility, per the rules.
  2. **Senior/Staff/Principal only** — Comcast/FreeWheel SWE 4, Upstart, ID.me Staff Testing & Evals, Capital One Full Stack Engineer 5, Affirm Staff, ATG Staff ×2, Mirantis Principal, GM Principal.
  3. **DevOps / SysAdmin / DevSecOps** — Accenture Federal, AnaVation ×2, CCS Global, NS2 Mission, Kohl's reliability. Excluded role families.
  4. **Not actually DC** — Chicago, Austin, Taunton MA, Minneapolis, Wisconsin, Texas appearing in a 30-mile DC search.
* **ZipRecruiter (DC, 30-mile, MID+JUNIOR, 21-day window, Go/Python/Kubernetes):** 5 of 328 returned, and the quality says everything — a **.NET** full-stack role, a customer-experience associate, an **IT help desk** specialist, a data-analytics analyst, and a community-college **web developer**. Zero relevant.
* **The structural conclusion, stated plainly:** a large share of DC-metro backend engineering sits behind **federal security-clearance requirements**. U.S. citizenship is confirmed for this candidate; **clearance status is not**, and the rules forbid assuming it. Everything else in that market skews senior or into operations. **This is why DC yields ~1 role per run, and it is a property of the market, not a gap in the search.** The honest recommendation: unless clearance eligibility can be established, DC should be treated as a one-or-two-company market (ID.me being the one), not as a metro worth a broad search each run.
* **ID.me (McLean, VA) is again the only DC-metro role that cleared the gate**, and it did so on requirements — 3+ years, Go/Python/TypeScript, Kubernetes, DevEx/platform scope — not on scarcity. Its dominant risk is the full-time-in-office rule against a stated unwillingness to relocate, which is a preference conflict to resolve with a recruiter before applying.
* **Not reached for DC:** DC-specific VC portfolio boards and DC startup career pages. Given the clearance structure above, I judge further DC breadth a poor use of budget — but it is a gap, not a conclusion.
