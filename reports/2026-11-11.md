## 🔧 What changed this run

**One methodology correction worth recording.** The previous run's note that Comeet serves a full `COMPANY_POSITIONS_DATA` array from any hosted page is **wrong as stated**, and following it would have produced false negatives. On a *single-position* page Comeet sets `COMPANY_POSITIONS_DATA = null` and only `POSITION_DATA` is populated. The full array is served from the employer's **careers landing position** URL (pattern `comeet.com/jobs/{company}/{uid}/careers/{x}`) — that call on Cyera returned **253 positions** with locations, `experience_level` and `time_updated`. Single-position pages remain perfect for *re-verification*; the careers-landing URL is what you need for *discovery*.

3. **Check Point — Full Stack Engineer (#1).** `2-5 years` with **Go** and **PostgreSQL** as stated mandatories — the single best alignment between a posting's required stack and this candidate's strongest evidence. Released 2026-10-28, so it is inside the responsive window.

Deliberately **not** on this list: GovEagle (#10), despite the strongest U.S. technical fit in the run. ## 🔹 Positioning Recommendation

Lead with: **Backend / Full-Stack Engineer with Go, Python and Kubernetes, building scalable cloud services and AI-enabled products.** Stigg's own wording — *"full-stack with a strong backend orientation"* — is the most useful phrase available and should be borrowed almost verbatim, because it claims the full-stack range without conceding the backend centre of gravity.

Three concrete proof points should appear in every application, because between them they satisfy the mandatory requirement of nearly every role above:

1. **Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway serving 100k+ requests/day at SAP** — carries Kubernetes, Docker, distributed systems, API infrastructure and production ownership in a single sentence.
2. **Production applications processing 100k+ documents/day at Qualtrics** — carries high-throughput pipelines, data-intensive systems and Python, and is the right answer to any "have you worked at scale" question.
3. **Root-cause analysis on production systems** — stated as a hiring criterion, not a nice-to-have, at GovEagle, Cyera and Upwind Research. Prepare one specific incident narrative: what broke, how it was traced, what changed so it could not recur.

Add the **MSc in Computer Science (UMD, 3.9)** where a posting names a degree — Upwind, Zero Networks, Cato and Check Point all do — and where teams are research-adjacent, as at Upwind Research and Pinecone. Add **U.S. citizenship, no sponsorship required** to every U.S. application, in the first line: it is a stated hard requirement at GovEagle and a silent filter nearly everywhere else.

**De-emphasise:** freelance teaching as a work-history block — it reads as a gap in full-time engineering experience and must not be counted toward the 2–3 years; redeploy it only as evidence of mentoring and technical communication, which Stigg, Cyera and Appdome all ask for explicitly. Also de-emphasise academic ML and data-science research when applying to product-engineering reqs: against the AI-platform roles it reads as depth, but against a backend req it invites the mis-read that the candidate wants a research seat. And do not lead with frontend or CSS work anywhere — not even on the full-stack reqs, where the backend half is what differentiates this profile.

A Lever sweep across twelve orgs returned nothing. **Wellfound, Startup Nation Central, Israeli VC portfolio boards and LinkedIn Jobs were not reached this run** — LinkedIn blocks automated fetch as expected, and the others were not budgeted after the ATS sweep proved higher-yield.

Ramp is the most under-examined NYC employer in this run and the first place to look next time.

**Washington, DC — searched actively, zero roles included, and the reason is structural.** Three independent sweeps were run: an Indeed connector search (Backend Software Engineer, Python/Go, Washington DC), a **Dice** search across a 30-mile radius sorted by posting date (637 full-time direct-hire matches enumerated), and a **ZipRecruiter** search filtered to MID and JUNIOR seniority with Go/Python/Kubernetes skills and a 45-day window (524 matches). The DC-metro result set is dominated by three categories, none of which can clear the gate:

1. **Cleared defence and intelligence work** — Wyetech (Annapolis Junction), Markon (Fort Meade), EpochGeo (TS/SCI), Boeing BI&A (Annapolis), CACI (TS/SCI with polygraph), General Dynamics (TS/SCI + poly, 10+ yrs), Base-2 Solutions (Top Secret/SCI, Bethesda), Vantor (TS/SCI with CI poly), BAE, Captivation, Clarity Innovations, CyberTrend. The candidate is a U.S. citizen and therefore clearance-*eligible*, but **holding an active clearance is not established by the Candidate Reference and must not be assumed** — and these postings require a clearance already held, often with polygraph. Correctly excluded as an eligibility condition that available evidence does not establish.
2. **Senior, lead, manager or architect scope** — Capital One's Full-stack Engineer 4 and 5 reqs are explicitly "(Manager, IC)" and "(Sr Manager, IC)" at $197k–$262k; General Atomics' Chantilly req is a "Backend/Fullstack Lead." All outside the brief.
3. **Staffing-firm and aggregator reposts** — Purple Drive, ARK Infotech, Impelsys, plus the Palantir record discussed above. Not verifiable as employer reqs.

Dice's own facets make the shape of this market explicit: of 637 matches, **450 On-Site, 2 Hybrid**, and **3 willing to sponsor**. The honest conclusion is that the DC metro's mid-level software-engineering market is structurally gated on security clearances the candidate cannot be shown to hold, and that **zero DC roles is the correct result** rather than a search failure. It is not worth a large share of future runs; one confirming sweep per run is proportionate.

**A general limit worth stating.** Where a posting does not state its work model, this report says "model not stated in posting" rather than inferring hybrid. Several Israeli employers above are widely understood to run hybrid schedules, but a widely-understood practice is not a verified term of the role, and inventing one would be the same class of error as inventing a link.
