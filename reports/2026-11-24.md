## 🔹 Positioning Recommendation

**Lead with:** *Backend engineer specialising in Go and Python on Kubernetes, with production experience in globally distributed, high-throughput cloud services — and a working track record in AI-enabled product engineering.*

The three assets to put in the first two lines of every application, because together they are rare at 2–3 years:
1. **Kubernetes controllers for a globally distributed API gateway, 100k+ requests/day.** Not "used Kubernetes" — *extended* it. This is the differentiator against every other mid-level backend applicant and it is the single most load-bearing line on the CV. It is what makes MongoDB (Atlas control plane), Lemonade (IaC-automated model deployment), Upwind and Stigg plausible despite their 3+ yr bars.
2. **A production pipeline processing 100k+ documents/day.** Use this wherever throughput, streaming or data-plumbing appears — Fullpath's CDP, Robinhood's Kafka/large-datasets bullet, Stigg's metering, Riskified's real-time ingestion.
3. **Evaluating and improving generative-AI systems.** This is the asset that lets a 2–3 year engineer compete for AI-platform roles asking 3–5 years (AppsFlyer, Writer, MongoDB Agent Engine). Most applicants have *used* LLM APIs; far fewer have built evaluation discipline around them. Pair it with the text analytics, classification and summarisation work.

**Secondary framing, for full-stack reqs only:** full-stack engineer with genuine backend ownership — React/TypeScript over Python services (the Qualtrics configuration). Use this for Connecteam, Fullpath, Remitly, Ramp and Check Point DLP. Do *not* lead with it for Robinhood, Upwind, Stigg or Lemonade.

**De-emphasise:**
- **Freelance / vocational teaching years.** Present them as evidence of communication, mentoring and technical explanation — never as engineering tenure. Any implication of a longer career than ~2–3 years will backfire in a reference or screening call, and the candidacy does not need it.
- **Academic ML and data-science research.** It supports the AI-platform narrative in one line; expanded, it reads as a research profile and invites the "wants to be a data scientist" misread that disqualifies backend candidates.
- **GPA and coursework.** The 3.9 MSc/BSc from UMD is worth exactly one line. Check Point, Cato, Lemonade and Appdome all state a degree requirement, so it clears a real bar — but it carries no weight past that.
- **Breadth-for-its-own-sake stack lists.** Depth in Go/Python/Kubernetes/AWS beats a long inventory. Several of these reqs explicitly screen for depth.

**Honest summary: Israel was searched comprehensively and productively. NYC was searched seriously and yielded one new qualifying role. Washington, DC was searched seriously for the first time with a positive result, but the DC market remains structurally hostile to this profile.**

### What was actually searched

**Israel (primary market — comprehensive).**
- Direct ATS API sweeps across **~90 company tokens** on Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever and SmartRecruiters, filtered programmatically to Israeli locations and non-senior engineering titles. Boards successfully retrieved and filtered: Connecteam, Riskified, Similarweb, Fireblocks, Island, Taboola, Bringg, Descope, JFrog, Orca Security, Tipalti, Forter, Via, Pagaya, Gong, Yotpo, Augury, Datarails, Dremio, Apiiro, Eon, Torq, Cymulate, Oasis, Sentra, Oligo, Sweep, Finout, Stigg, Lemonade, Cato, AppsFlyer, Check Point (full Israel board, 41 reqs).

**New York City (secondary — serious, strict gate applied).** Ashby and Greenhouse board sweeps across ~60 U.S. company tokens; ATS-domain-restricted web searches; re-verification of all five previously reported NYC roles via Ashby/Greenhouse APIs. One new role cleared the gate (Robinhood).

**Washington, DC (secondary — serious, strict gate applied).** Greenhouse/Ashby sweeps across ~35 DC-metro and DC-adjacent company tokens (Appian, Virtru, Huntress, Expel, Vannevar Labs, HawkEye 360, Figure, Primer, Quorum, Morning Consult and others), filtered to DC/VA/MD locations; Indeed DC search; **Dice** DC search (611 full-time direct-hire results surveyed by facet); **ZipRecruiter** DC search filtered to junior/mid seniority. One role cleared the gate (Remitly, Arlington).

### What blocked, failed, or came up empty

- **LinkedIn Jobs — not retrievable.** As in every prior run, LinkedIn blocks automated fetch. Mitigated by pivoting to underlying company postings, but **Israeli roles advertised only on LinkedIn are a standing blind spot** and are likely the largest single category of missed opportunity in this report.
- **JS-rendered ATS pages return titles only.** Direct fetches of Ashby, MongoDB and Cato posting pages returned the job title and nothing else. **Worked around** by using the Greenhouse and Ashby public board APIs, which return full structured JSON — this is now the primary verification path and is more reliable than page fetching. Cato's page fetch returned empty content entirely; it was verified via the Greenhouse API instead.
- **Lever API returned "Document not found" for all ~30 tokens probed.** Either those companies do not use Lever or the public posting endpoint is unavailable from this environment. **Lever-hosted Israeli and U.S. roles are therefore unsearched this run** — a real gap, since some Israeli startups do use Lever.
- **Comeet-hosted Israeli startups are under-covered.** Comeet is heavily used in the Israeli market and its API requires per-company tokens that cannot be enumerated. Comeet-hosted companies were reached only when already known by URL (Fullpath, Appdome, Upwind) or surfaced by search. **This is the most significant structural gap in the Israel search.**
- **~55 of ~90 probed ATS tokens returned 404** (Wiz, Snyk, Gong's main board, Cyera, Armis, Outbrain, HiBob, Rapyd, monday.com, Next Insurance, Placer, Verbit, Deel, Coralogix and others). These companies are hiring but on boards this run could not address. Not evidence of no openings — evidence of no access.
- **Web search was low-yield this run.** General queries returned Glassdoor/Workopia/CrawlJobs/Built In aggregator pages with unreliable dates and mostly senior roles. Two useful leads came from it (Connecteam, Robinhood); both were then verified independently via ATS API. **No role in this report rests on a search snippet.**
- **Not reached at all:** Wellfound, Startup Nation Central, Israeli VC portfolio boards (Insight, Qumra and similar appeared in search results but were not systematically swept), and Israeli tech-community boards (nodetlv, Secret Tel Aviv). Budget went to ATS sweeps instead, which have a far better verification-per-call ratio — but these remain unsearched.
- **Dice and ZipRecruiter for Israel:** deliberately not used, per the operating instructions. Both were used for the U.S. markets.

### Why Washington, DC produced only one role

DC was searched properly and the result is structural, not a sampling artefact. Across 611 Dice full-time direct-hire results and ~35 DC-metro ATS boards, essentially everything fell into four buckets that disqualify it:

1. **Clearance-gated.** TS/SCI or Top Secret requirements dominate the DC engineering market — Esri (Backend SWE, TS/SCI), Virtru (Forward Deployed, clearance required), Capgemini/FBI, Leidos, Vantor. The candidate's clearance eligibility is **not** established by the Candidate Reference, so these are excluded as **unknown-to-negative eligibility risk** rather than assumed either way. U.S. citizenship is established and is necessary but not sufficient for a clearance.
2. **Senior/Staff.** Okta (Staff SWE, DC), Capital One (Senior Lead, Distributed Systems, Golang+Python on Kubernetes — a near-perfect technical match at entirely the wrong level), Affirm (Staff, Resilience), Two Six (Senior Full Stack), Leidos (SWE SME).
3. **Entry-level or not software engineering.** Appian's McLean "Product Engineer" is an *Associate* Product Engineer — React/JavaScript UI work configuring a low-code automation platform, onsite 5 days. Excluded on three separate grounds (entry-level, frontend/low-code, and not backend engineering). Also surveyed and excluded: DevSecOps and Kubernetes-ops contract roles, a Linux sysadmin req, and a software developer internship.
4. **Federal contracting generally**, where the hiring process and domain are a poor fit for this profile.

**Remitly is the exception that proves the rule:** a commercial product company opening a *new* Arlington hub, with no clearance condition, a requirement list naming Go and Python, and a hybrid model — and it was posted yesterday. That is the shape of the only DC roles worth the candidate's time, and they are rare. Previous runs in this series correctly reported zero DC roles; today's result reflects a genuinely new posting, not a loosened bar.
